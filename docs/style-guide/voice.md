# Voice and writing

Two voices. **The log** (narration, events) is the captain writing in the evening: first person plural, past tense, plain and specific, a light period flavour. **The interface** (buttons, stats, tooltips) is modern, short and exact.

## The log

- Specific over atmospheric: "Water casks at a third. The men are quiet." not "A sense of unease settles over the ship."
- Period flavour through vocabulary (leagues, soundings, stores, the hands), never through spelling. No "ye", no "olde", no pirate talk.
- Day stamp first: "Day 14. Birds at dawn, heading south-west."
- Two to four sentences per entry.

## The interface

- Buttons are verbs that say what happens: "Set sail", "Send a shore party", "Turn for home", "Sell chart", "Keep secret".
- An action keeps its name everywhere: the button "Keep secret" produces the note "Kept secret: Cape Hollin".
- Sentence case. Numbers as numerals with units: "18 days", "40 tons", "£1,200".
- Consequences are stated, not implied: "Cut rations: +5 days range, sickness more likely."
- Warnings say what and why: "Provisions reach the point of no return in 2 days."

## Peoples of the new lands

The people the player meets are communities with their own names, interests and leverage, never scenery or obstacles. Use their names for themselves and their places, shown alongside any name the player gives. No caricature in words or art; write their choices with the same seriousness as the Admiralty's.
